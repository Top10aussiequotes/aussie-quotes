The 10 Greatest Australian Quotes

A cozy look at the fireside wisdom, gritty resilience, and brilliant wit that defined the Australian spirit.

#1
"Such is life."
Ned Kelly
Said just before his execution at the Old Melbourne Gaol, 1880.
#2
"And the bush hath friends to meet him, and their kindly voices greet him in the murmur of the breezes and the river on the bars."
A.B. "Banjo" Paterson
From the iconic poem 'Clancy of the Overflow', 1889.
#3 • Forgotten Gem
"Us mortals have no place in the business of bureaucrats."
William Campbell
Stated upon concealing Victoria's first major gold discovery to protect pastoralists from government interference, 1850.
#4
"All her years of solitary life and hardship have not crushed her spirit."
Henry Lawson
From 'The Drover's Wife', capturing the resilience of outback women, 1892.
#5
"Any boss who fires a worker for not turning up today is a bum."
Bob Hawke
The Prime Minister's live television reaction after Australia won the America's Cup, 1983.
#6
"We are all visitors to this time, this place. We are just passing through. Our purpose here is to observe, to learn, to grow, to love... and then we return home."
Aboriginal Proverb
A traditional philosophy of deep spiritual connection to Country.
#7
"I don't think there's any risk of it going to my head. You can't get big-headed around Australian cricket fans."
Sir Donald Bradman
Reflecting on the grounded nature of Australian sports culture.
#8
"We’ll make the tyrants feel the sting of those that work and fight."
Henry Lawson
From the political labor poem 'Freedom on the Wallaby', 1891.
#9
"The actual danger is nothing, and the positive advantages very great."
William John Wills
Written in his journals during the fateful Burke and Wills expedition, 1861.
#10
"That's not a knife... THAT'S a knife."
Paul Hogan
As Mick 'Crocodile' Dundee in the global blockbuster film 'Crocodile Dundee', 1986.

Bringing forgotten Australian history back to light, one cozy campfire story at a time.
